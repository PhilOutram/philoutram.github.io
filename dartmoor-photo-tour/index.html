Two days covers it

Shooting for the poster look

Low sun

Flat-colour posters rely on clear shapes. The first and last two hours of daylight give long shadows and strong silhouettes; midday light flattens tors into grey lumps.

Big shape, big sky

Keep the horizon low and leave empty sky at the top of the frame for the title. Shoot portrait as well as landscape at every stop.

Find a leading line

A path, river, wall or railway that runs into the picture does the work the stream does in a classic beach poster.

Add something for scale

A walker, a pony or sheep makes a tor read as big. Keep them small and simple.

Check the weather twice

Conditions on the high moor change fast and differ from Exeter. Take a paper map: phone signal is patchy.

Seasons to aim for

Gorse is yellow most of the year and brightest in spring; bluebells at Emsworthy in mid-May; heather purple from August.
